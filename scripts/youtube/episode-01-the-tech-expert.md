# Episode 01: The Tech "Expert"

**Genre**: Workplace Comedy
**Length**: 20-30 minutes
**Theme**: Humility through Technology Mishaps
**Lesson**: Pride comes before the fall

---

## ENGLISH VERSION

### CHARACTERS

- **DIRECTOR PARK** (50s) - The stubborn, know-it-all boss who claims to be a tech expert
- **JENNY** (28) - Young, competent marketing manager, often dismissed by Director Park
- **MIKE** (25) - IT support specialist, frequently ignored
- **SUSAN** (45) - Senior employee who's seen it all
- **MR. ANDERSON** - Important client (voice/brief appearance)

---

### ACT 1: THE SETUP

**SCENE 1 - MONDAY MORNING, OFFICE MEETING ROOM**

*Director Park stands at the head of the conference table, arms crossed, looking smug. The team sits around nervously.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(condescending tone)
People, people, people. I've been in this industry for 30 years. THIRTY. YEARS. I was sending emails when you were still in diapers.

**JENNY:**
(trying to be helpful)
Director Park, about the client presentation on Friday, I think we should—

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(interrupting, waving hand dismissively)
Jenny, Jenny, Jenny. Did I ask for your opinion? When I need advice from someone who watches TikTok all day, I'll let you know.

*Jenny slumps back in her chair, exchanging glances with Mike.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(puffing chest out)
I've personally set up the new presentation system for Friday's meeting with Anderson Corp. Cost us $50,000. State of the art. Cloud-based. AI-integrated. Blockchain-enabled.

**MIKE:**
(raising hand tentatively)
Sir, I don't think blockchain is relevant for a presentation sys—

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(cutting him off)
Mike, Mike, Mike. This is why you're in IT support and I'm a director. You fix printers. I make strategic decisions. There's a difference.

**SUSAN:**
(diplomatically)
Perhaps we should have a backup plan? Just in case—

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(laughing condescendingly)
Backup plan? Susan, I appreciate your years of service, but this isn't the 1990s anymore. Everything is in THE CLOUD now. Nothing can go wrong. I've personally configured everything. And unlike some people here, I actually READ the manual.

*He holds up a thick manual, clearly still in its plastic wrapping.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(continuing)
The manual is right here. Not that I need it. I'm something of a tech expert myself.

---

**SCENE 2 - WEDNESDAY, JENNY'S DESK**

*Jenny and Mike are having coffee.*

**MIKE:**
He didn't even open the manual, did he?

**JENNY:**
(sighs)
The plastic wrap is still on it. And did you hear him? "Blockchain-enabled presentation system." That's not even a thing!

**MIKE:**
I tried to check the system yesterday. He changed all the passwords and won't share them. Says he doesn't trust us with "critical infrastructure."

**SUSAN:**
(walking over)
Oh, you two. I've worked with five directors before Park. They all have their... quirks. He'll learn eventually.

**JENNY:**
But the Anderson presentation is our biggest deal this year! If something goes wrong—

**SUSAN:**
(knowingly)
Then he'll learn faster.

---

### ACT 2: THE FALL

**SCENE 3 - FRIDAY MORNING, 9:00 AM - DIRECTOR PARK'S OFFICE**

*Director Park sits at his desk, looking confident. He's rehearsing his presentation.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(to himself)
"And as you can see, Mr. Anderson, our ROI projections are..."

*He clicks his mouse. Nothing happens. He clicks again. Still nothing.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
What the...

*He jiggles the mouse. Clicks frantically. Types on keyboard.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(starting to sweat)
This is fine. This is totally fine.

*He picks up the phone and dials.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(into phone, trying to sound calm)
Mike? Yes, hi. Can you come to my office? Immediately? It's... urgent.

---

**SCENE 4 - DIRECTOR PARK'S OFFICE, 9:15 AM**

*Mike enters. Director Park is standing, trying to look casual but clearly stressed.*

**MIKE:**
You called, sir?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(awkwardly)
Yes, Mike. I need you to... assist me with a minor technical matter. Very minor. Insignificant, really.

**MIKE:**
What seems to be the problem?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(defensive)
There's no PROBLEM. I'm simply... delegating. Yes. I'm delegating this task to you. The system appears to be... temporarily unresponsive.

**MIKE:**
(sitting at computer)
Okay, let me see... Hmm. Sir, it's asking for a password. Multiple passwords, actually. Looks like you set up multi-factor authentication?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(proudly)
Of course I did. Security first! That's what the manual recommended. Page 47.

**MIKE:**
Did you... read page 47?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
I skimmed it. The important parts.

**MIKE:**
Okay, what's the password?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(confidently)
It's "DirectorPark2024" with a capital D and P.

*Mike types. Error sound.*

**COMPUTER:**
"Incorrect password. 2 attempts remaining."

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(nervous laugh)
Try "DirectorPark2024!" with an exclamation point. For security.

*Mike types. Error sound.*

**COMPUTER:**
"Incorrect password. 1 attempt remaining. Account will be locked after next failed attempt."

**MIKE:**
(concerned)
Sir, we need to be careful. One more wrong attempt and—

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(panicking slightly)
I KNOW what it means! I set it up! Try... try "TechExpert2024"

*Mike types reluctantly. Error sound.*

**COMPUTER:**
"Account locked. Please contact system administrator."

**MIKE:**
(sighs)
And who's the system administrator?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(quietly)
...I am.

**MIKE:**
Sir, the client presentation is in 3 hours.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(voice rising)
I'm AWARE of that, Mike!

---

**SCENE 5 - OFFICE, 10:00 AM**

*Director Park has called an emergency meeting. He's sweating. Jenny, Mike, and Susan sit across from him.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(trying to maintain dignity)
So, team. I've decided to... test your problem-solving skills. This is a training exercise.

**JENNY:**
(skeptical)
A training exercise?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
Yes. I've intentionally locked myself out of the presentation system to see how the team responds under pressure. Very strategic. Very forward-thinking.

**SUSAN:**
(not buying it)
Is that so?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(sweating more)
Absolutely. So... any ideas? Purely theoretical, of course.

**MIKE:**
Well, there's a factory reset option, but that would delete all your settings and the presentation files.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(horrified)
DELETE? No, no, no. Next option.

**JENNY:**
Did you write down the passwords anywhere?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(indignant)
Write them down? That's Security 101! Never write down passwords! I'm not an amateur, Jenny.

**JENNY:**
(patiently)
Okay. Did you use the password manager app Mike recommended last month?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(dismissive)
Password managers are for people who can't remember things. I have an excellent memory. I just... temporarily can't access it right now.

**SUSAN:**
What about the recovery email?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(mumbling)
I used my old company email. From my previous job. That closed down five years ago.

*Everyone stares at him.*

**MIKE:**
Sir, with all respect, when I offered to help you set this up last week—

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(defensively)
You were going to do it all wrong! With your... your simple passwords and basic security!

**JENNY:**
Director Park, the presentation is in 2 hours and 45 minutes.

---

**SCENE 6 - OFFICE, 11:00 AM - MONTAGE**

*Series of quick scenes showing various failed attempts:*

1. Director Park trying to kick the computer (doesn't work)
2. Mike on phone with tech support, being put on hold
3. Jenny searching online forums for solutions
4. Susan making calming tea for everyone
5. Director Park pacing, checking his watch repeatedly
6. Mike trying different password combinations, all failing

---

**SCENE 7 - OFFICE, 11:45 AM**

*Everyone looks defeated. Director Park is sitting with his head in his hands.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(quietly)
This is a disaster. The biggest client meeting of the year, and I've ruined it.

**SUSAN:**
(gently)
What time is Mr. Anderson arriving?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
12:30. Forty-five minutes.

**JENNY:**
(tentatively)
Director Park... I have a suggestion.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(looking up, desperate)
Anything. I'm listening.

**JENNY:**
Last week, I created a backup presentation. Just in case. It's on my laptop. It's not as fancy as your cloud system, but it has all the data, projections, and slides we need.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(stunned)
You... you made a backup?

**JENNY:**
Yes. I know you said not to, but I thought... well, better safe than sorry.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(emotion in voice)
Jenny, I... I told you not to waste time on backups.

**JENNY:**
I know. I did it on my lunch break.

**MIKE:**
(chiming in)
And sir, I can set up the old projector system in Conference Room B. It's not as high-tech, but it's reliable. I can have it ready in 20 minutes.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(looking at Mike)
The system you recommended I keep as backup? The one I called "obsolete technology"?

**MIKE:**
(nodding)
That's the one.

**SUSAN:**
(standing up)
And I'll contact Mr. Anderson and give him the new room number. I have his personal cell phone - we play golf together sometimes.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(surprised)
You play golf with Anderson?

**SUSAN:**
(smiling)
Yes. You never asked.

---

### ACT 3: THE LESSON

**SCENE 8 - CONFERENCE ROOM B, 12:25 PM**

*Mike has set up a simple but functional presentation system. Jenny's laptop is connected. Everything is ready. Director Park stands at the front, looking humbled.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(to the team)
Before Mr. Anderson arrives, I need to say something.

*The team looks at him.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(genuine)
I've been... I've been a fool. A proud, stubborn fool.

**JENNY:**
Sir, you don't have to—

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(continuing)
No, I do. Jenny, you showed initiative and wisdom when I showed arrogance. Mike, you offered help that I was too proud to accept. Susan, you have relationships and skills I completely overlooked.

*He pauses, emotional.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
I've been so focused on proving I'm the smartest person in the room that I stopped listening to the actually smart people in the room. You three just saved my career, and probably this company.

**MIKE:**
(kindly)
We're a team, sir. That's what teams do.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(nodding)
A team I haven't been treating like a team. I've been treating you like... like...

**SUSAN:**
Underlings?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(wincing)
Yes. And I'm sorry. Truly sorry.

*There's a knock on the door.*

**JENNY:**
That'll be Mr. Anderson.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(to Jenny)
Jenny, would you... would you co-present with me? This is your backup plan. You should share the credit.

**JENNY:**
(surprised)
Really?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
Really. And Mike, can you stay in case there are any technical questions?

**MIKE:**
(grinning)
Happy to help, sir.

**SUSAN:**
(opening door)
I'll bring in our guest.

---

**SCENE 9 - CONFERENCE ROOM B, 1:30 PM - AFTER PRESENTATION**

*Mr. Anderson is shaking hands with Director Park and Jenny.*

**MR. ANDERSON:**
Excellent presentation. I'm particularly impressed with your team's clear communication and collaborative approach. You've got the contract.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(humbly)
Thank you, Mr. Anderson. But the credit really goes to my team. They're the reason this company works.

**MR. ANDERSON:**
(nodding approvingly)
A leader who acknowledges his team. That's refreshing. I'll have my people send over the contracts next week.

*Mr. Anderson leaves. The team celebrates quietly.*

---

**SCENE 10 - DIRECTOR PARK'S OFFICE, NEXT MONDAY**

*Director Park is in a meeting with Jenny, Mike, and Susan.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
So, I've been doing some thinking over the weekend. Mike, I'd like you to take full control of all our IT infrastructure. Including passwords. All of them.

**MIKE:**
(pleased)
Thank you, sir. I'll set up a proper system with shared access and secure recovery options.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(to Jenny)
Jenny, I want you to lead the next client presentation. From start to finish. I'll support you, but it's your show.

**JENNY:**
(excited)
I won't let you down!

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(to Susan)
And Susan, I'd like to have lunch with you this week. I clearly don't know half of what goes on around here, and I suspect you know everything.

**SUSAN:**
(amused)
I know where all the bodies are buried, if that's what you mean.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(laughing)
That's exactly what I mean.

*He stands up and looks at his team.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
I spent 30 years thinking experience meant having all the answers. Last Friday taught me that real experience means knowing when to ask for help. And who to ask.

**MIKE:**
Sir, if you don't mind me saying... you're still going to be the tech expert who locked himself out of the system at the next company party, aren't you?

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(grinning)
Probably. But now I'll laugh about it with you instead of pretending it didn't happen.

**JENNY:**
That's growth.

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
(nodding)
That's humility.

---

**FINAL SCENE - OFFICE, LATER THAT WEEK**

*Director Park is at his desk. The still-wrapped manual sits in front of him. He picks it up, looks at it, then walks over to Mike's desk.*

**DIRECTOR PARK:**
Mike, would you help me actually read this manual? I have a feeling I might learn something.

**MIKE:**
(smiling)
I'd be happy to, sir.

*They sit down together. Camera pulls back to show the office, now with a better atmosphere.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Director Park learned that being the smartest person in the room means nothing if you're too proud to listen. Real leadership isn't about having all the answers—it's about building a team that does.

**[END]**

---

## MORAL OF THE STORY

Pride and arrogance blind us to the talents and wisdom of others. True humility comes not from avoiding mistakes, but from acknowledging them, learning from them, and valuing the people who help us grow.
